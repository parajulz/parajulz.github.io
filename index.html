Targeting the THRα–ADR–BCL6 Axis in Cardiac Regeneration
Used Geneformer — a transformer pre-trained on millions of single-cell transcriptomes — to perform in silico perturbations on Thyroid Hormone Receptor Alpha (THRα) and adrenergic receptors across global human heart data, measuring regulatory effect on BCL6, a transcriptional repressor linked to cardiomyocyte cell cycle arrest.

Key finding: β-adrenergic signaling via ADRB1 is the dominant regulatory axis. ADRB1 deletion caused a measurable BCL6 embedding shift across 6,954 cells (cosine sim: 0.9741), while THRα alone showed zero detectable effect — suggesting adrenergic signaling, not thyroid hormone, is the primary modulator here.

Autonomous Vehicle Perception for Off-Road Environments
Off-road autonomous perception is a fundamentally harder problem than highway autonomy: terrain is unpredictable, lighting is inconsistent, and the cost of a missed obstacle is physical. This project tackled that directly.

Developed CNN-based multi-sensor fusion models combining camera and depth data for simultaneous obstacle detection and terrain classification. The core challenge was sensor disagreement — when camera and depth inputs conflict under adverse conditions, naive fusion fails. The model was designed to learn when to trust which sensor and how to weight conflicting signals.

Architected a real-time PyTorch inference pipeline optimized for deployment on resource-constrained hardware, cutting latency 40% through model pruning, quantization-aware training, and batched inference scheduling. Conducted systematic failure mode analysis across diverse terrain datasets — mud, gravel, vegetation, slopes — identifying the specific conditions under which the model degraded and retraining to close those gaps. Hyperparameter tuning across terrain types brought false positive rate down 18%.

Reading — My All Time Faves ↓
The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo
Stieg Larsson
Top-peak thriller. Once it grabs you, it doesn’t let go. I lost sleep over this one.
Pedagogy of the Oppressed
Paulo Freire
Freire doesn’t give you answers — he dismantles the questions you thought you already had. Read slowly. Sit with it.
The Master and Margarita
Mikhail Bulgakov
Surrealist Soviet satire. The devil visits Moscow and only chaos follows. A strange masterpiece.
Never Let Me Go
Kazuo Ishiguro
Ishiguro writes grief without naming it. The characters accept what they can’t change with a stillness that cuts deeper than rage — the kind of stillness you only reach when you’ve looked at something long enough to stop running from it. It asks what it means to live fully inside a life that was never yours to design, and somehow finds grace there.
The Secret History
Donna Tartt
You know the ending from page one. What Tartt builds instead is the slow archaeology of how beautiful people become capable of terrible things — and how easy it is to be complicit in something you never fully understood.
Giovanni’s Room
James Baldwin
Baldwin writes shame the way it actually lives — quiet, structural, inherited. Every sentence carries weight that most writers spend whole careers trying to find. A book that knows you better than you’d like.
Never Lie
Freida McFadden
A twist that genuinely earns it. Once you see it, you can’t unsee it. I finished it in one sitting and felt mildly disturbed for a day.
